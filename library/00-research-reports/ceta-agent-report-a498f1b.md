### UK: no ratification vote required
CRaG 2010 s.20 is a **negative** procedure. The treaty is laid for **21 sitting days**; only a **Commons** resolution against ratification blocks it (renewable indefinitely); a **Lords** resolution is not binding — the Minister may proceed with an explanatory statement. **No affirmative vote, and no Act of Parliament, is needed.** Here no resolution against ratification was moved; the period simply expired on 5 March 2026. **No implementing primary legislation** was passed — everything was done by SI under existing enabling Acts.

**UK implementing secondary legislation (all now verified):**
- **SI 2026/36 — The Customs (Tariff and Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026** (Taxation (Cross-border Trade) Act 2018, ss.9(1), 11, 17(6)–(7), 32(7)–(8)). Reg 3(2) inserts into the table in **Schedule 1 to SI 2020/1457** (Customs Tariff (Preferential Trade Arrangements) (EU Exit) Regulations 2020), immediately above the Israel entry: col.1 the CETA; col.2 "**The India Preferential Tariff, version 1.0, dated 13th January 2026**"; col.3 "**The India Origin Reference Document, version 1.0, dated 13th January 2026**". Reg 3(2) commences **on EIF**; Reg 1(3) obliges the SoS to publish a **London Gazette** notice of the EIF date. ## TOPIC B — RULES OF ORIGIN

Primary sources: CETA Chapter 3 — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-india-ceta-chapter-3-rules-of-origin (PDF: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/687e58acae8555bab409a070/uk_india_ceta_chapter_3_rules_of_origin.pdf); Annex 3A PSRs — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/687e5e378adf4250705c96db/uk_india_ceta_annex3a_product_specific_rules_of_origin.pdf; Modalities — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a561263822fbe6b8245c8ba/modalities-of-the-authentication-process-for-origin-declarations.pdf; India ORD v1.0 — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/696f485a2b64f0e8c32e3333/The_India_Origin_Reference_Document_v_1.0.docx

### 1. General rule — there is **no single residual rule**
Art 3.2: a good originates if (a) **wholly obtained**; (b) produced entirely from originating materials; or (c) produced using non-originating materials and satisfying **Annex 3A**. Footnote 2: final production must occur in the exporting Party.

Annex 3A is **exhaustive chapter-by-chapter**; there is no catch-all residual. The **de facto default is "CTSH and Standard QVC"** (37 chapter-level occurrences), with **"CTH and Standard QVC"** next (29). Annex 3A is on **HS 2022** (Headnote 10). Where alternatives are given, meeting any one suffices; where requirements are cumulative all must be met.

**"Standard QVC" (Headnote 9(f)) = not less than:**
- **(i) 40% of the ex-works price** under build-down; **or**
- **(ii) 45% of the FOB value** under build-down; **or**
- **(iii) 35% of either ex-works price or FOB value** under build-up.

Art 3.4: value of the good may be **either ex-works price or FOB**. Art 3.5 formulae: **Build-Down** = (value of good − value of non-originating materials)/value of good × 100; **Build-Up** = value of originating materials/value of good × 100. Art 3.6 gives **roll-up/absorption**. Art 3.13: indirect materials are neither originating nor non-originating for QVC.

### 2. Sector PSRs (exact, from Annex 3A)
- **Textiles and apparel, Ch 50–63 (incl. 61, 62, 63): "CTH and Standard QVC"** — **single transformation plus a value test, NOT EU-style double transformation**. No textile section note. Fabric can be imported and made up into garments provided the QVC is met.
- **Footwear, Ch 64: "CTH" only — no QVC.** The single most liberal rule in the Annex.
- **Leather:** Ch 41 CTSH + Standard QVC; **Ch 42 (travel goods, handbags, leather articles) CTH + Standard QVC**; Ch 43 CTH + Standard QVC.
- **Gems and jewellery — Ex Ch 71 CTSH + Standard QVC, but with very low bespoke QVCs:**
 - **7102 diamonds: CTSH + QVC ≥ 6%**
 - 7103 precious/semi-precious stones: CTSH + QVC ≥ 20%
 - 7106 silver: CTSH + QVC ≥ 25%
 - 7110 platinum: **CC**
 - **Ex 711311 silver jewellery: plain ≥ 3.5%; studded with gemstones ≥ 6%; studded with diamonds ≥ 7%; parts ≥ 3.5%** (all CTSH +)
 - **Ex 711319 gold jewellery: plain ≥ 3.5%; studded with gemstones ≥ 6%; with diamonds ≥ 7%; with pearls ≥ 6%; parts ≥ 3.5%**
 - 711320 base-metal clad ≥ 3.5%; 7114 goldsmiths'/silversmiths' wares ≥ 3.5%
 - **7117 imitation jewellery: CTSH + QVC ≥ 35% EXW build-down / 40% FOB build-down / 30% build-up**
 - 7118 coin: CTH + QVC ≥ 1.5%
 → i.e. India's cut-and-polish and jewellery-making value-add of a few per cent is deliberately enough.
- **Marine:** Ch 3 (fish, crustaceans) **WO**; Ch 16 (preparations of fish/crustaceans) **CC**. The WO rule for Ch 3 means imported raw fish cannot be re-exported as Indian-origin.
- **Chemicals/pharma:** Ch 28 and 29 and **Ch 30 pharmaceuticals = "CTSH; or Standard QVC; or chemical reaction"**; Ch 32 same; Ch 33 "CTH; or chemical reaction; or mixing and blending"; Ch 34, 36 "CTH; or chemical reaction"; Ch 35 "CTSH; or chemical reaction"; Ex Ch 31 fertilisers "CTSH; or chemical reaction", 3105 "CTH; or chemical reaction". Section Note 1 = Chemical Reaction Rule; Section Note 2 = Mixing and Blending Rule.
- **Auto components:** **8706/8707/8708 = "CTH; or QVC ≥ 50% EXW build-down / 55% FOB build-down / 45% build-up"**; 8714 parts CTH or QVC ≥ 50/55/40; **Ex Ch 87 generally CTH + QVC ≥ 45/50/40**; **8703 motor cars = "a QVC of not less than 35%"** with no CTC; 8711 motorcycles QVC ≥ 50/55/40; 8716 CTSH + Standard QVC.
- **Engineering goods:** Ex Ch 84 CTSH + Standard QVC (8407 "CTH; or no change in tariff…"); Ex Ch 85 CTSH + Standard QVC (8501, 8502 "CTH; or Standard QVC"); Ch 86 CTSH + Standard QVC; Ch 88 "CTH; or Standard QVC"; Ch 89 CTSH + Standard QVC; Ex Ch 90 CTSH + Standard QVC.
- **Iron and steel: Ex Ch 72 = "Melt and Pour in the Parties"** (7201–7205 CTH + QVC ≥ 50/55/45); **Ch 73 CTH + QVC ≥ 50%**. This is the tightest rule in the Annex and matters given the separate UK steel quota measure.
- Others: Ch 1–5 WO; Ch 17 Ex CC (1702/1704 CTH); 4001 WO; Ch 44–49 and 65–68 CTSH/CTH + Standard QVC; **Ch 69 ceramics = CC**; **Ch 70 glass = CTH**.

### 3. Tolerance / de minimis (Art 3.9; India Rule 10)
- **HS Ch 1–3, 5, 6, 10, 14 → 7.5%** of value **or** net weight
- **HS Ch 4, 7–9, 11–13, 15–24 → 12.5%** of value **or** net weight
- **HS Ch 25–98 → 12.5% of value only**
Tolerance applies to CTC and WO rules only, **not** to QVC; and Art 3.9(2) confirms non-originating value still counts toward any QVC test. Note 6 of the Annex headnotes allows tolerance to override chapter exclusions.

### 4. Cumulation, non-alteration, drawback
- **Art 3.8: bilateral cumulation only.** No diagonal, no extended, no third-country cumulation. Indian exporters cannot count Bangladeshi, Chinese or EU content.
- **Art 3.14 non-alteration:** goods must remain under **customs control** in any non-Party; only unloading, reloading, splitting, separation from bulk, storing, labelling, marking, **bottling** or preservation permitted. Footnote 5: bottling covers **HS 2208 from bulk only**; a non-manipulation certificate may be required where bottling occurs in a non-Party.
- **No prohibition on duty drawback.** Grep of Chapter 3 and Annex 3A returns nothing; the only drawback reference in Chapter 2 is permissive (temporary admission: goods "conditionally relieved … **or eligible for duty drawback**"). **Indian exporters can therefore stack drawback/RoDTEP with CETA preference** — a material advantage over EU-style FTAs.

### 5. Proof of origin — asymmetric (Art 3.15–3.18; India Rules 16–19)
**Into the UK (Indian exports) — three routes:**
1. **Origin declaration** completed by the exporter or producer (Annex 3B template)
2. **Certificate of origin** issued by an Indian issuing authority (Art 3.17; India exchanges names and specimen seals with HMRC)
3. **Importer's knowledge**

**Into India (UK exports) — one route only:** an **origin declaration by the exporter or producer**. **No certificate of origin and no importer's knowledge.** Backed by a mandatory bilateral **authentication** process (Art 3.16.7, Annex 3D, Annex 3E data protection).

Common terms: **valid 12 months** from completion/issue; must be **in writing including electronic**, **in English**, accompanied by an invoice/commercial document; **one shipment** — except that **for imports into the UK only**, a declaration may cover **multiple shipments of identical goods within a period not exceeding 12 months**. Third-country invoicing is allowed. Retrospective completion is allowed marked "**completed retrospectively**" with explanation. Force majeure acceptance after expiry.

**Art 3.18 low-value waiver: UK only — customs value ≤ £1,000** (or a higher figure the UK specifies). To be reviewed at the first Working Group meeting. **India has no equivalent waiver** — every consignment into India needs a declaration and authentication regardless of value.

**Art 3.20 refunds/late claims:** no later than **one year** after importation (or longer under the importing Party's law). **Art 3.22:** minor errors are not grounds for rejection; **30 days** to supply a corrected proof.

### 6. Registration and the authentication mechanics
**UK exporters (for exports to India):**
- Register **once** with HMRC using your **EORI** (GB or XI prefix) — not per shipment. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/register-to-complete-origin-declarations-under-the-uk-india-free-trade-agreement (first published 15 January 2026; from July 2026 the service also allows changing or cancelling registration)
- Primary email **plus up to 10 additional** = **max 11 email addresses per EORI**; the same email cannot be registered against two EORIs (doing so causes CBIC rejection). Allow **up to 24 hours** after registering.
- HMRC pushes EORI + emails to CBIC in **JSON over TLS 1.2+** via an API endpoint.
- Per shipment: complete the Annex 3B template, convert to a **single PDF**, and email it **from a registered address** to **cbic.customs.indiaukceta@CBICIndia.onmicrosoft.com** **and** the Indian importer in the same email. **Subject line format `EORI-DDMMYYYY`** (signature date on the declaration), e.g. `GB123456789-01/07/2026`. **Multiple attachments trigger automatic rejection.** One email per shipment; a declaration cannot be reused.
- CBIC returns a **Unique Reference Number (URN)** to the exporter, copying the importer. If authenticity is not established the importer **cannot** claim preference. CBIC "shall not determine that authenticity is not established solely on the basis of **minor discrepancies**." Authentication is **not** a claim and does **not** confirm the goods meet the rules of origin; no penalty attaches solely to a failed authentication.
- Declarations must be **dated on or after 15 July 2026** or may be rejected.
- Outage: notify without delay; late claims under Art 3.20 on restoration; if unavailability exceeds **21 days**, refer to the Subcommittee on Trade in Goods for an alternate mechanism.
- Modalities **signed in two originals at New Delhi on 9 July 2026** by **Lindy Cameron** (British High Commissioner) and **Vivek Chaturvedi** (Chairman, CBIC); "These modalities shall constitute an **integral part of the CETA**." UK nodal address: **customsauthorisations@hmrc.gov.uk**.

**Indian exporters (for exports to the UK):** the **signatory's reference number** on the Annex 3B declaration is the **IEC (Importer Exporter Code)** (for UK signatories it is the HMRC-assigned exporter reference number). Since 15 July 2026, preferential CoOs and declarations are filed and issued electronically on the **Trade Connect ePlatform / eCoO 2.0 at trade.gov.in**, by **either self-declaration or an authorised agency** listed in **Appendix 2B** (DGFT Trade Notice 11/2026-2027; PN 11/2026-27). **There is no "approved/registered exporter" scheme on the Indian side** — India did not build a registration analogue to HMRC's.

### 7. Record-keeping, verification, denial, suspension
- **Art 3.24 / Rule 25:** importer **≥ 4 years** from importation; producer/exporter **5 years** from issuance/completion (in India this also binds the issuing authority). gov.uk operational guidance states 5 years for UK exporters.
- **Art 3.25 / Rule 26 verification sequence:** request to importer first → written request to the exporting Party's competent authority **no later than 2 years** after the claim → acknowledgement within **30 days** → verification report within **7 months** (+3 by mutual agreement) → verification-visit request within **30 days** of the response, response within **45 days**, **21 days'** notice, up to **2 observers** from the importing Party (government officials, acting through the exporting Party's authority) → information within **45 days** of the visit → determination within **1 year** of receiving sufficient information. Verification may be risk-based, random, or intelligence-led. Goods may be released against duty payment or security.
- **Fraud/collusion/wilful misstatement/suppression:** request within **5 years**, response within **12 months** (+6 by agreement), relying on the **UK–India Agreement on Cooperation and Mutual Administrative Assistance in Customs Matters, signed in London 31 May 2021**. Requests from the UK go to the **Department of Commerce** in India.
- **Art 3.19:** grounds for denial of preference (including that goods entering or released into customs control on or after EIF are eligible — the basis for the in-transit transition, India Rule 20(1)).
- **Art 3.26 temporary suspension of preferential treatment:** exporter-specific after **2 failed verifications**; **sector- or good-wide** where 2+ exporters have each failed twice and collectively account for the **majority** of exports of that good in the preceding 12 months; **15 days'** prior notification; consultations via the Working Group; compliant exporters may be exempted. This is the sharpest tail-risk for concentrated Indian sectors (gems, textiles).
- **Art 3.1 definitions:** competent authority — India: **Department of Commerce** (exports) / **CBIC** (imports); UK: its customs authority. Territorial sea = 12 nm. **Art 3.27** confidentiality. **Art 3.28** Working Group on Rules of Origin — meets within 12 months of EIF, then annually, reporting to the Subcommittee on Trade in Goods.

### 8. Carve-outs Indian exporters should watch
1. **Ch 3 marine is WO** — imported raw fish cannot be re-exported; only Ch 16 processing (CC) tolerates non-originating raw material.
2. **No diagonal cumulation** — Bangladeshi fabric, Chinese components and EU inputs are all non-originating.
3. **Steel is "Melt and Pour"** and Ch 73 needs CTH + 50% QVC — plus the separate UK steel quota measure (188 lines, 51% volume cut, 50% out-of-quota tariff, 40% per-country cap) sits on top and is not a rules-of-origin issue.
4. **8703 cars need a 35% QVC with no CTC route** — pure assembly of imported CKD will fail.
5. **Tolerance never rescues a QVC failure**, only CTC/WO rules.
6. **Into India, only a self-completed origin declaration works**, and only if the UK exporter has pre-registered with HMRC and the email/subject-line/single-PDF mechanics are exactly right. Operationally this, not the substantive rules, is where preference will most often be lost.
7. Rule 20(1) transition: goods entering or released into UK customs control **on or after 15 July 2026** may claim preference even if shipped earlier (PDEXCIL advisory reading of Notification 62/2026-Customs (N.T.)).
