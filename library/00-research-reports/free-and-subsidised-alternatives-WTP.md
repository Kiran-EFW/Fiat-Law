# FREE / SUBSIDISED ALTERNATIVES TO A PAID UK-ENTRY BUNDLE FOR INDIAN CORPORATES
## Competitor research, August 2026 — and the willingness-to-pay verdict

**Method note.** WebSearch was not used (blocked per your instruction). SearXNG was tested and discarded as contaminated. Everything below came from direct URL fetches, GOV.UK's search and content APIs, sitemap and CDX grepping, Wayback retrieval for bot-walled sites, PDF extraction, and Companies House. Three sites (grow.london, cii.in, britishbusinessbank.co.uk) are bot-walled; the workaround that beat Cloudflare was the reader proxy `https://r.jina.ai/<URL>`, which returns HTTP 200 full markdown. ## HEADLINE ANSWER, UP FRONT

The free ecosystem is large, well-funded, India-focused, and **compresses willingness-to-pay by roughly 80–100% on the advisory and orientation layer, and by approximately zero on the execution layer.**

Across roughly 30 organisations examined — DBT, the Office for Investment, London & Partners, six regional agencies, Innovate UK, the British Business Bank, UKIBC, CII, FICCI, ASSOCHAM, NASSCOM, TiE, the Indian High Commission, Invest India, Startup India and STPI — **not one will incorporate a company, be a registered office, act as a director, sign a Companies House filing, prepare statutory accounts, give regulated legal advice, or accept liability.** Every single one stops at the introduction and disclaims. That is not an oversight; it is structurally forced by MLR 2017, ECCTA 2023, FSMA and the Companies Act, and it is why the boutique's product cannot be given away by the state.

The real competitive threat is not that the free bodies will do your work. It is that **DBT now trains every inbound Indian corporate to expect a free first hour with an actual professional firm, and hands them a pre-curated shortlist of your competitors.** Your funnel, not your fees, is what is under attack.

# PART 2 — WHERE THE MONEY ACTUALLY IS

## The three-tier test: which visa routes force a UK footprint

This is the sharpest commercial finding in the whole exercise.

**Tier A — nothing UK-side required at application.** Global Talent (no entity, no address, no sponsor, no job offer). Innovator Founder at *first* application — Appendix Innovator Founder INNF 8.1–8.3 imposes no Companies House requirement. **These routes bypass you entirely.**

**Tier B — UK entity deferred but unavoidable.** Innovator Founder at extension/settlement. **INNF 9.5, verbatim: "The applicant's business must be registered with Companies House and the applicant must be listed as a director or member of that business."** Incorporation is postponed ~3 years, not avoided, and endorsing-body checkpoints at 12 and 24 months police progress.

**Tier C — hard UK footprint and UK-based people.** All sponsored routes. Sponsor guidance Part 1, verbatim:
- **L8.3:** "If you have no operating or trading presence in the UK (or, in the case of UK Expansion Worker, if you have no UK footprint), we will refuse your application."
- **L4.33:** "Your Authorising Officer must be a paid member of staff or an office holder within your organisation. **You cannot appoint a representative or somebody from another organisation to fill this role.**"
- **L4.20:** "The representative must be based in the UK – you cannot appoint an overseas-based representative for any Key Personnel role."
- **L8.20:** virtual/serviced-office business models trigger a compliance visit to the Authorising Officer's physical address.

**Two commercial consequences.** (a) **A nominee director cannot be sold as the Authorising Officer** — do not market it that way; it will fail and it will fail loudly. (b) A UK registered address **is** mandatory and **is** physically inspectable, so your registered-office product is squarely in scope but must be a real, inspectable address, not a mailbox.

**UK Expansion Worker is your single best product-market fit.** Sponsor a Global Business Mobility Worker, v04/26, verbatim **GBM3.4**: "You must, however, show you have a UK 'footprint'. You can show this by providing evidence you have either: **UK business premises** – for example, a lease agreement...; **registered your UK business with Companies House** as either an overseas company branch or a new company that is a subsidiary of the overseas linked business – in this case, **you need only provide your Companies House reference number**."

Read that again. A £100 incorporation is the literal entry ticket to a route that unlocks tens of thousands of pounds of relocation. The Home Office itself flags incorporation as the low-friction option. **No free programme supplies either a lease or an incorporation.** Other constraints: GBM3.3 "you must not already be actively trading in the UK"; GBM3.6 overseas business trading ≥3 years; GBM3.26 CoS capped at 10 (capped at **1** if the Authorising Officer is overseas); GBM3.29 **Provisional** rather than A rating if the AO is overseas; GBM3.37 licence valid 4 years, non-renewable; GBM3.38 full UK trading presence within 2 years or CoS allocation drops to zero.

## The regulatory moat — why free bodies legally cannot cross the line

**MLR 2017 / TCSP supervision.** Per gov.uk, a Trust or Company Service Provider is a business that will "form companies or other legal persons"; "sell off-the-shelf firms"; "provide a: registered office, business address, correspondence address" and an "administrative address"; "act or arrange for another person to act as a: director or secretary of a company"; act as "nominee shareholder for another person". **"You must not run your business until HMRC has confirmed that your application has been successful."** HMRC fees from 1 Dec 2025: **£300 non-refundable application; £400 per premises registration; £400 per premises annual declaration; £500 per person fit and proper test (applies to TCSPs); £40 per person approval check.**

Every one of the four core items in your bundle — incorporation, registered office, nominee director, corporate secretarial — sits inside this definition. A public agency or a chamber of commerce cannot offer them without becoming an HMRC-supervised TCSP with fit-and-proper-tested officers. **They will never do this.**

**ECCTA 2023.** "From 18 November 2025, identity verification becomes a legal requirement", with a 12-month transition. Directors supply a personal code at incorporation and with the next confirmation statement; PSCs within specified 14-day windows. An overseas company "must confirm that all directors have verified their identity by the anniversary of your UK establishment's registration." Routes: GOV.UK One Login (biometric passport from any country accepted), Post Office, or an **ACSP** "such as an accountant or solicitor". Critically: third-party filers "will need to register their business as an **Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP)**" to file for clients. **ACSP status is a licence to be in your business. No free body holds it.**

**Registered office "appropriate address."** Verbatim from changestoukcompanylaw.campaign.gov.uk: "Companies must, at all times, have an 'appropriate address' as their registered office"; a document delivered there "would be expected to come to the attention of a person acting on behalf of the company" and "the delivery of documents there is capable of being recorded by the obtaining of an acknowledgement of delivery"; "**You're not able to use a Royal Mail PO Box, and equivalent services offered by other parties.**" Using an agent is allowed but "you must make sure the address service they provide meets the requirements." Failure consequences: "We may take action against the company and its officers as they may be committing an offence"; Companies House will "change the registered office address to a default address"; the company must "provide an appropriate address with evidence of proprietary ownership within 28 days" or "we could start the process to strike the company off the register"; plus financial penalty, register annotation, or prosecution.

**Companies House fees** (guidance updated 2 July 2026): incorporation **£100** digital/software, £124 paper, same-day £156 software; **registration of a UK establishment of an overseas company £124 (PAPER ONLY)**; confirmation statement **£50** digital / £110 paper; change of name £20/£30, same-day £85; voluntary strike off £13/£18; Register of Overseas Entities registration £250/£528, annual update £134/£268.

**An honesty point you need to hear.** Under the Legal Services Act 2007 s.12 only six activities are reserved: rights of audience, conduct of litigation, reserved instrument activities, probate activities, notarial activities, administration of oaths. **General commercial legal advice is NOT reserved in England and Wales — anyone may give it.** So "we give legal advice and they can't" is a weak claim and a sophisticated Indian GC will see through it. Your actual defensible moat is: HMRC AML/TCSP supervision, ACSP status, willingness to hold officer and signatory roles, professional indemnity insurance behind contractual liability, statutory accounts, audit (registered auditors only), and FSMA authorisation for M&A arranging and advising.

## The honest summary

**The free ecosystem has driven the price of *knowing what to do* to zero. It has not moved the price of *someone doing it and being answerable for it* at all.**

Three things follow, and two of them are bad news.

**Bad news 1 — your top of funnel is under real attack.** DBT's Investment Support Directory hands every inbound Indian corporate a curated list of 21 incorporation firms, 19 immigration firms, 18 accounting firms and 14 M&A firms, and promises them a **free first hour** from each. L&P offers free triage across "banking, accounting, legal and immigration". You cannot charge for scoping, discovery workshops, market-entry reports, or a paid first meeting. Anything in your commercial model that depends on being paid to explain the UK is dead. Structure engagements so that revenue starts at execution.

**Bad news 2 — the sub-£300 commodity floor is real and visible.** A competent Indian CFO can Google their way to a £199.99 non-resident incorporation package with a registered office and a director service address for about £300 in year one. Against that, an unexplained five-figure "UK entry bundle" looks like rent-seeking. You must price the *liability and the officer roles*, not the filings, and you must say so explicitly.

**Good news — the moat is statutory, not rhetorical, and it is getting deeper.** ECCTA made identity verification mandatory from 18 November 2025 and made ACSP registration a prerequisite to file on a client's behalf. The "appropriate address" rules killed PO-box registered offices and attached strike-off risk. MLR 2017 makes registered-office provision, company formation, and arranging directors or nominee shareholders into HMRC-supervised activities with fit-and-proper-tested officers. **Public agencies and chambers of commerce cannot enter this space, and every one of them has published wording confirming they have not.** The regulatory burden that annoys you is the reason your revenue exists.

## Precisely which paid services the free alternatives CANNOT substitute for

1. **Incorporation as an executed, warranted service** — including the ECCTA identity-verification chain, correct PSC and director statements, and getting the branch-vs-subsidiary decision right. Note that a UK establishment registration is **£124 and paper-only**; nobody free will handle it.
2. **A registered office that is a genuine "appropriate address"** — capable of acknowledging delivery, not a PO box, inspectable by Home Office compliance officers under L8.20, and carrying strike-off exposure if it fails.
3. **Director, secretary, officer and signatory roles.** With the caveat that these are real directorships with real Companies Act duties — Elemental CoSec's public position that "there is no such concept as a nominee director" in UK law is correct — and that a nominee **cannot** be the sponsor licence Authorising Officer (L4.33).
4. **Authorised Corporate Service Provider filing on the client's behalf**, plus confirmation statements, PSC maintenance and the whole ongoing Companies House relationship.
5. **Statutory accounts, corporation tax, VAT, PAYE and payroll** — regulated work; audit reserved to registered auditors.
6. **Regulated M&A arranging and advising** — an FSMA-authorised activity that no free body could perform if it wanted to.
7. **Contractual and professional liability backed by PII.** This is the actual product. L&P: "L&P accepts no liability arising from the supply of any such goods and/or services by our partners." SDI: "does not constitute advice... seek the advice of an appropriately qualified person." Wales: "Listing should not be taken as endorsement of any kind." WMGC: "we disclaim all liability and responsibility arising from any reliance placed on such materials", plus a client indemnity. Marketing Manchester: "Under no circumstances will Marketing Manchester be liable for... loss of business... loss of opportunity." Enterprise Oxfordshire: "**We cannot confirm that they are legally entitled or professionally qualified to carry on a particular trade, business or profession and bear no liability for merely supplying their details.**" DBT: "being listed in the Directory does not create legal relations between the member and DBT."
8. **Sponsor licence preparation and the UK footprint that unlocks it.** GBM3.4 makes a Companies House reference number or a lease the entry ticket to the UK Expansion Worker route, and no free programme supplies either. OfI fast-tracks the queue for a tiny gated cohort but still requires the applicant to produce a plausible costed 12-month operating plan.
9. **Confidentiality on live transactions.** SDI states in writing that its site "is not a secure means of communication" and that clients "should not submit or send" confidential information. Free agencies are structurally unable to hold deal-sensitive material.

## Three practical implications

**Put the disclaimers on one slide.** L&P clause 2.3 ("Any support provided to you is for information purposes only"), SDI ("does not constitute advice... seek the advice of an appropriately qualified person in your home jurisdiction"), and Enterprise Oxfordshire ("We cannot confirm that they are legally entitled or professionally qualified"). Three public agencies, in their own words, telling your prospect that they will not stand behind anything. That slide is your entire pricing justification.

**Treat the free agencies as channel, not competition.** The DBT Investment Support Directory is free to list on. L&P sells Grow London Member and Growth Partner status. Wales runs a Soft Landing Directory. WMGC runs Partners West Midlands. Invest Manchester publishes £4k/£8k/£16k tiers. Redfern Legal, Oury Clark, IMA and GoGlobal are already inside L&P's directory doing your bundle. You are not on any of these lists.

**Two whitespaces worth noting.** Goodwille, the closest full-bundle competitor, covers nine European countries and **not India**. And West Yorkshire — where the mayor has run repeated India trade missions and welcomed an Indian High Commission delegation — has **no inward-investment service at all**: whyleeds.com is an expired parked domain and there is no investor offer in the entire 1,601-URL combined-authority sitemap.
