# Indian & Indo-British trade bodies / free soft-landing programmes — India→UK market entry
Research completed August 2026. All figures verified in-session. "Not published" means I looked at the relevant pages and it genuinely is not there.

**Method note:** WebSearch was not used (blocked). Everything below came from direct URL fetches, sitemap/CDX grepping, Wayback Machine retrieval (for Incapsula/Cloudflare-walled sites), PDF extraction, and UK Companies House lookups. Where a live site was blocked I say so and name the archive snapshot.

---

## HEADLINE VERDICT

**Not one of these organisations delivers UK-side operational services.** Zero offer UK incorporation, registered office, nominee/resident directorship, company secretarial or accounting. What they all sell is advocacy, events, delegations, B2B matchmaking, reports — and visa *recommendation letters*. The single productised "landing pad" in the whole set (UKIBC Launchpad®) runs **UK→India**, the wrong direction.

The decisive competitive fact: **CII's UK-corridor delivery is visibly outsourced to Grant Thornton.** The "India Meets Britain Tracker" is "developed in collaboration with the Confederation of Indian Industry" (https://www.grantthornton.co.uk/insights/india-meets-britain-tracker/). 2024 = 11th edition, 12 Jun 2024: "a record 971 Indian-owned business operating in the UK, up from 954 the previous year", employing "over 118,000 people across the country"; top-100 combined turnover £42.8bn, average growth 48%, corporation tax £473.6m, capex £1.4bn. Grant Thornton monetises it through its South Asia Business Group, International Expansion, International Business Centre and Global Compliance Reporting Solutions. **CII has no UK-registered legal entity at Companies House** (swept: only unrelated CIIQ/CIIB/CIIC names). So the incumbent price anchor for India→UK operational work is professional-firm rates, not membership rates. Membership does not compress WTP for regulated work — it compresses WTP for introductions and credibility only.

## 10. WHAT NONE OF THEM DO — the commercial opening

Across every organisation above, the following are **universally absent**:
- UK company incorporation
- Registered office / service address
- Nominee, resident or corporate directorship
- Company secretarial and Companies House filing
- UK statutory accounts, VAT, PAYE, payroll
- Legal advice of any kind
- M&A advisory
- Any signatory, fiduciary or liability-bearing role

The only operational touchpoint any of them offers is a **visa recommendation letter** (FICCI up to 50/yr; CII "Visa facilitation / Passport Endorsement Service"; ASSOCHAM "Complimentary VISA Recommendation Letters").

**An important nuance on disclaimers:** none of these bodies carries an explicit "we do not provide legal advice" or "seek independent professional advice" clause of the kind the brief anticipated. That is because **none of them purports to advise at all** — CII and FICCI's disclaimers are generic website-accuracy/no-liability clauses, NASSCOM has no disclaimer page at all (404), ASSOCHAM has none, and UKIBC's is an anti-fraud notice. The gap in the market is **structural, not contractual**. That is a stronger position for a paid bundle than a disclaimer-based one would be: there is no ambiguity to argue about.

**Effect on willingness to pay:** membership fees are cheap relative to professional fees (CII International Member US$1,500–3,000/yr; FICCI Corporate ₹7 lakh ≈ £6,500; TiE London Corporate £2,000+VAT), but they buy an entirely different good. The realistic WTP compression is on *market intelligence, introductions and events* — not on regulated or operational delivery. The pricing anchor that matters is Grant Thornton's, not CII's.
