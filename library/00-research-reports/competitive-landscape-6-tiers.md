# UK MARKET-ENTRY SERVICES FOR INDIAN COMPANIES — COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE
**As at 4 August 2026. All figures verified from live pages this session; URLs given throughout.**

## VERDICT UP FRONT

The market is **crowded at both ends and empty in the middle**. There is a commodity floor at £100–£300 that sells filings and an address, and an unpriced professional ceiling that sells advice by the hour. Between them sits a band — roughly £3,000 to £25,000 per engagement — where an Indian mid-cap needs someone to *actually execute and sign*, and there is no branded, priced, India-facing provider in it.

Three facts carry the whole analysis:

1. **Goodwille** ([goodwille.com/services](https://www.goodwille.com/services/)) already sells the exact bundle — subsidiary registration, company secretarial, registered office, mail forwarding, agent for service, bookkeeping, payroll, HR, sponsorship support, CFO. It has dedicated country pages for **Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Netherlands, Norway and Sweden**. India is not one of them. The model is proven; it is simply pointed at Scandinavia.
2. Across **~35 UK law firms** examined, firms offering registered office: **0**. Nominee/provided directors: **0**. Accounting: **1**. Published pricing: **0**.
3. Across **~25 government and trade bodies**, not one will incorporate a company, be a registered office, act as a director, sign a filing, or keep books. Every one stops at the introduction and disclaims liability.

**Demand context** (Grant Thornton *India Meets Britain* 2026, 13th edition, [grantthornton.co.uk/news-centre/india-meets-britain-2026](https://www.grantthornton.co.uk/news-centre/india-meets-britain-2026/)): **1,912 Indian-owned UK companies, up ~60% year on year**; combined turnover £105.77bn (from £72.14bn); 203,549 UK employees (+60.6%); bilateral trade £47.4bn in 2025. Anuj Chande OBE cites GT's own IBR: **99% of Indian firms with a UK presence plan to expand, and ~90% of those not yet here intend to establish a base.** CETA and the Double Contributions Convention both entered into force 15 July 2026.

## TIER 3 — INDIAN LAW / ADVISORY FIRMS WITH UK PRESENCE

### The regulatory reality that shapes this tier
**BCI Rules for Registration and Regulation of Foreign Lawyers and Foreign Law Firms in India, 2022** (notified 10 Mar 2023), **substantially replaced by gazette notification of 13 May 2025**. Foreign firms may advise on foreign/international law and appear in international arbitration (narrowed by the 2025 amendment); they may not advise on Indian law, appear before Indian courts or conduct litigation. Fly-in fly-out is capped at **60 consecutive days in any 12-month period** with a mandatory Form C. Case spine: *Bar Council of India v. A.K. Balaji* (SC, 13 Mar 2018); rules under challenge in *Narendra Sharma v. BCI*, WP(C) 1556/2024 (Delhi HC, not stayed). **Uptake: only three foreign firms registered in India as of April 2025.**

The mirror constraint is the commercially important one: **Indian firms cannot practise English law.** Any Indian firm marketing "UK entry" is either referring the regulated work to an English firm, or running a non-SRA UK entity limited to unreserved activities.

### How much does free provision compress willingness to pay?
**Severely, but in exactly one layer.**

- **Advice and orientation: compressed ~80–100%.** Market intelligence, sector data, site search, hosted visits, salary benchmarking, introductions to banks and recruiters, grant navigation, a named account manager, and — via the ISD — **a free first hour with a real professional firm**. Charging for a "UK market entry report" or a "discovery workshop" is dead on arrival.
- **Execution: compressed ~0%.** Not one of ~25 organisations will incorporate a company, be a registered office, act as a director, sign a filing, keep books, or accept liability. Every single one stops at the introduction and disclaims in terms.
- **Why they structurally cannot cross that line:** MLR 2017 makes registered-office provision, company formation, and acting as or arranging a director or nominee shareholder a **TCSP activity requiring HMRC supervision** (£300 application, £400/premises, £500/person fit-and-proper); **ECCTA** made identity verification a legal requirement and restricts third-party filing to **Authorised Corporate Service Providers**; the registered office must be an "appropriate address" capable of acknowledging delivery, with strike-off risk; audit is restricted to registered auditors; and M&A arranging is FSMA-regulated. Public bodies will not take that on.

**Net:** WTP for advice is near zero. WTP for **signature, filing, address, officer roles and liability** is uncompressed, and is anchored by professional-firm rates, not membership rates.

## WHERE IS THE GAP?

### Honest answer first: much of this market is crowded, and three ideas in the original bundle are weak.

**Do not lead with incorporation.** It is a £99.99 product sold by ACSP-registered agents with better UX than any law firm will build. Selling it at a professional margin is a losing argument you will have on every call.

**Do not lead with M&A advisory.** Grant Thornton, BDO, Menzies, Blick Rothenberg and Moore Kingston Smith all have real corporate finance teams, and arranging deals engages FSMA. A boutique's mid-market M&A claim is unsupportable against that bench and, on the numbers, is the least frequent event in a client's lifecycle.

**Do not sell "nominee director" as a product name.** Elemental CoSec — the most sophisticated operator in this space — publicly rejects the concept: UK law "is very clear that there is no such concept as a nominee director." A UK company **does not require a UK-resident director**. Marketing one as a requirement is misleading. There is no reduced liability in English law (full CA 2006 ss.171–177 duties apply), instructing the appointee risks shadow directorship under s.251, and "rent-a-director" D&O is a standard market exclusion — possibly uninsurable at sensible cost. The SRA's own TCSP guidance names formation + registered office + nominee director + accounting sold as a bundle to non-resident owners as the classic laundering typology and will supervise accordingly.

**Also be clear-eyed that:** free triage is genuinely good and genuinely free (L&P, Invest NI, SDI, DBT), Deel will legally employ your client's first two UK staff for $1,198/month with no entity at all, and the "we'll guide you into the UK" consultancy pitch is already occupied by anonymous operators competing on price.

### Now the whitespace — five things, in order of defensibility.

**1. Lawful immigration advice is a hard regulatory moat, and the corridor's boutiques cannot cross it.**
Giving immigration advice or services in the UK without OISC registration or an SRA/BSB exemption is a **criminal offence under s.84 Immigration and Asylum Act 1999**. UKSaarthi names no regulator. Singhania & Co UK states it is not SRA-regulated. Lex Meridian discloses nothing. Albertson, gunnercooke and Zaiwalla do not do immigration at all. Meanwhile **UK Expansion Worker is the route by which an Indian parent seeds a UK subsidiary**, and it requires a UK footprint the client does not yet have — which means immigration and incorporation are literally the same problem, and almost nobody sells them as one transaction. Only **Child & Child** and **Greenwich Solicitors** name the specific routes; Greenwich names no lawyers at all.

**2. The sponsor licence Key Contact / Level 1 User slot is solicitor-only and nobody is selling it.**
A sponsor licence needs an Authorising Officer, a Key Contact and Level 1 Users. The Key Contact and Level 1 User roles can be filled by a UK-based **legal representative** — and only a regulated one. This is a recurring, annual, compliance-bearing relationship with a genuine regulatory barrier, sold to a client who has no UK staff yet. Statutory costs are public and unavoidable: Home Office **£1,682** Worker licence (**£611** small/charitable), **£1,071** to add Worker to a Temporary licence, **+£750** priority (decision in 10 working days), **£525** per Certificate of Sponsorship, Immigration Skills Charge **£1,000/yr** (**£364** small), IHS **£1,035/yr**. **No adviser in this market publishes its own fee for this work.**

**3. ECCTA/ACSP identity verification is a compliance deadline with a structural cost advantage for an SRA firm.**
Identity verification became a legal requirement from 18 November 2025, with the transition closing 18 November 2026. Every director and PSC of those 1,912 Indian-owned UK companies — most of them resident in India — needs verifying, and third-party filers must be Authorised Corporate Service Providers. An SRA-regulated firm is **already AML-supervised** and therefore does not need separate HMRC TCSP registration (which otherwise costs £300 + £400/premises + £500/person). That is both a cost advantage and a trust story: *"your directors are in Pune and need verified UK identities before November."* This is a hard, dated, non-optional event with a defined population.

**4. Published fixed prices would make you unique. Literally unique.**
Zero of ~35 law firms publish a price. Zero of eight accountancy firms publish a price. Elemental, Goodwille, Oury Clark, TMF, Vistra, Ocorian and Suntera all publish nothing. The only published prices in the entire corridor are the £99–£300 commodity agents and Coddan's £1,399–£1,899. **An Indian founder cannot obtain an indicative UK-entry cost from any professional firm's website in this market.** Publishing "UK Entry: £X" — formation + registered office + first-year cosec + PAYE/VAT + sponsor licence application + first CoS — converts the free-first-hour problem into an advantage: DBT's ISD sends you a lead expecting a free hour; you spend the hour confirming a price the client already saw.

**5. The seam between the lawyer and the accountant is unowned, and Oury Clark proves it can be closed.**
Penningtons — the best mid-market inbound practice in the country — explicitly refers accounting out to Indian firms. Child & Child's accounting is one in-house accountant filing tax returns. Every other law firm punts entirely. So the client runs two suppliers, and the handoff between the person who forms the entity and the person who has to file for it is managed by nobody. **Oury Clark** (SRA 266280, solicitors + accountants + FCA under one brand, since 1935) is the structural precedent — and it targets **Australia, New Zealand, US and Canada**, not India. An ABS licence is the route to doing this properly in one regulated entity.

### The single sentence that describes the gap
**Goodwille's product, Oury Clark's structure, Child & Child's immigration depth, and a published price — aimed at India.** Nobody has assembled those four, and each one exists separately and demonstrably works.

### What the offer should therefore look like
Regulated-execution-first, not advice-first. Sell: (a) the sponsor licence and Expansion Worker route, priced; (b) the incorporation and ACSP identity verification as a compliance deliverable with a date, not a consultancy engagement; (c) registered office and cosec as the annuity; (d) accounting as the retained line; (e) legal advice as the thing that makes (a)–(d) lawful to sell together. Give the market-entry advice away — it is already free from DBT, L&P and Invest NI, and charging for it marks you as the fifth-cheapest consultancy rather than the only regulated one.

**Positioning line implied by the evidence:** *the only SRA-regulated firm that will incorporate your UK company, verify your Indian directors, get your sponsor licence, hold your registered office and file your accounts — at a published price.*

1. **MHA** — inbound-explicit, names immigration + entity setup + banking + PAYE/VAT on one page, Baker Tilly India behind it. The benchmark.
2. **Child & Child** — the deepest immigration list in the corridor plus in-house accountancy; vulnerable in that the desk is fronted by a non-partner "Client Lead" and offers no registered office, cosec or director capability.
3. **Elemental CoSec** — has the entire corporate-services stack including resident directors and ACSP verification; vulnerable in that it steers to SPVs and holdcos, offers no immigration and no regulated legal advice, and publishes no price.
