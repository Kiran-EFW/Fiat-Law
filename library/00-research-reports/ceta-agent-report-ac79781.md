## 1. TCSP STATUS — MLR 2017

### The definition (verbatim, reg 12(2))

> "'trust or company service provider' means a firm or sole practitioner who by way of business provides any of the following services to other persons, when that firm or practitioner is providing such services—
> **(a)** forming a firm;
> **(b)** acting, or arranging for another person to act— (i) as a director or secretary of a company; (ii) as a partner of a partnership; or (iii) in a similar capacity in relation to other legal persons;
> **(c)** providing a registered office, business address, correspondence or administrative address or other related services for a company, partnership or any other legal person or legal arrangement;
> **(d)** acting, or arranging for another person to act, as— (i) a trustee of an express trust or similar legal arrangement; or (ii) a nominee shareholder for a person other than a company whose securities are listed on a regulated market."

Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/12. Limb (a) was substituted (from 1.9.2022) by SI 2022/860 reg 4(2) — "forming a firm" is wider than "forming companies"; "firm" in MLR reg 3 means any entity that is not an individual. **There is no limb (e).**

**Answers to your three sub-questions — all three are TCSP activity:**

| Proposed service | Caught by |
|---|---|
| (b) Company incorporation | reg 12(2)(a) |
| (d) Registered office / mailbox / virtual office | reg 12(2)(c) |
| (c) Nominee / provided director | reg 12(2)(b)(i) — note "**or arranging for another person to act**", so brokering a third-party nominee is equally caught |

HMRC guidance confirms mailbox, virtual office and mail-forwarding services to corporate customers are in scope, and that from a **30 June 2026** guidance update, selling **off-the-shelf/ready-made companies** is expressly TCSP activity (a firm not formed on a specific customer instruction). Source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-register-for-money-laundering-supervision-if-youre-a-trust-or-company-service-provider

### Who supervises — and the critical point on the sister company

- MLR reg 7 allocates supervision. **HMRC ("the Commissioners") is the default supervisor for TCSPs not supervised by the FCA or a professional body supervisor.** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-who-needs-to-register
- Reg 54(2): HMRC **must** maintain a register of all TCSPs not on the FCA register — this includes TCSPs supervised by a professional body. So SRA-regulated firms doing TCSP work **still appear on HMRC's TCSP register**, but they get there via the SRA: notification on form FA10/FA10b, SRA applies the reg 58 fit-and-proper test to the firm, its beneficial owners, managers and officers, and recommends to HMRC, which is the final decision-maker. "No contact with HMRC is needed." Source: https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/resources/money-laundering/trust-company-service-provider-guidance/
- **FIAT LAW UK LTD is not an SRA authorised body and is not an SRA-regulated freelance solicitor. It is therefore outside SRA supervision entirely and must obtain its own HMRC registration.** The SRA route is available only to entities the SRA can authorise. This is the single clearest compliance requirement in the whole package.
- Frequency is irrelevant: the SRA guidance states it "does not matter how infrequently you might do TCSP work, or whether it might be provided as ancillary" to other services.

### Registration, cost, fit-and-proper, penalties

- **Reg 56(1): a person must not act as a TCSP unless registered.** The transitional carve-out in reg 56(2)(b) applies only to applications made before 10 January 2020. HMRC guidance: "You must not run your business until HMRC has confirmed that your application has been successful." Approval is a **pre-condition of trading**, unlike most other HMRC-supervised sectors.
- **Fit and proper test — reg 58.** Reg 58(3): a Schedule 3 conviction means automatic failure. Reg 58(4) factors: past MLR compliance failures; the ML/TF risk of the business; and whether the applicant and any officer, manager or beneficial owner "has adequate skills and experience and has acted and may be expected to act with probity."
- **Fees (HMRC, effective 1 December 2025):** £300 one-off non-refundable application fee; £400 per premises on registration; £400 per premises annual declaration; **£500 per person fit-and-proper test (applies to TCSPs)**; £40 per person approval check (accountancy sector). Small-business reduction available under £5,000 turnover. Source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-registration-fees
- **Penalties.** Criminal: MLR reg 86(1) — summary conviction up to **3 months' imprisonment and/or a fine**; on indictment up to **2 years' imprisonment and/or a fine**. Reg 86(3) provides a "all reasonable steps and all due diligence" defence. Civil: HMRC penalty plus a **£2,000 sanction administration charge** for failing to register; since 1 December 2025 that charge also applies where HMRC suspends or cancels registration, censures, or imposes a prohibition on management. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-appeals-and-penalties

### Knock-on effect

Registration makes FIAT LAW a "relevant person" under MLR reg 8, triggering the **full** regime: firm-wide risk assessment (reg 18), policies/controls/MLRO and nominated officer (regs 19–21), CDD/EDD (regs 27–35 — EDD is likely mandatory for many India-based structures), record-keeping (reg 40), training (reg 24), plus POCA 2002 SAR obligations.

**Risk flag:** the SRA's own TCSP guidance warns that offering a wide range of in-scope services makes a provider look like "a one-stop shop for laundering the proceeds of crime." Formation + registered office + nominee director + accounting, sold as a bundle to non-resident owners, is *precisely* the FATF/HMRC high-risk profile. Expect intensive supervision.

## 2. ECCTA 2023 — THE ACSP REGIME

### What an ACSP is

- **CA 2006 s1098A** (inserted by ECCTA s.66): an ACSP is a person whose application to the registrar has been granted under s1098B, who has not ceased (s1098F) and is not suspended.
- **CA 2006 s1098B — the eligibility gate:**
  - s1098B(1)(a): the applicant **must be "a relevant person as defined by regulation 8(1) of the Money Laundering Regulations"** — i.e. **AML supervision is a statutory pre-condition**;
  - s1098B(1)(b): an individual applicant's identity must be verified (s1110A);
  - s1098B(2)(a): the application must name the supervisory authority;
  - s1098B(3): the registrar **must check with the supervisory authority** that the applicant is known to and supervised by it;
  - s1098B(5): the registrar **must refuse** if the applicant is "not a fit and proper person to carry out the functions of an authorised corporate service provider."

**Commencement:** ss.1098A–1098H inserted 26.10.2023 for regulation-making purposes only, **fully in force 18 March 2025** (ECCTA ss.66(4), 219). Further ACSP detail was added by **S.I. 2025/1037** (amending s1098C required information) — I have not read that SI; recommend checking it.

### Precise IDV mechanics now live

- **Directors:** supply the Companies House personal code with the company's **next confirmation statement** — separately for each directorship. When commenced, s1067A(2) will restrict filing on behalf of a firm to: (i) a verified officer/employee of the firm; (ii) a verified officer/employee of a corporate officer of the firm; or (iii) **an ACSP**. Companies House guidance (updated June 2026) still lists filers under "at a later date."

## 3. REGISTERED OFFICE POST-ECCTA

**CA 2006 s86** (substituted by ECCTA s.28(3); in force **4 March 2024**, SI 2024/269 reg 2(v)):

> "(1) A company must ensure that its registered office is at all times at an appropriate address.
> (2) An address is an 'appropriate address' if, in the ordinary course of events—
> (a) a document addressed to the company, and delivered there by hand or by post, would be expected to come to the attention of a person acting on behalf of the company, and
> (b) the delivery of documents there is capable of being recorded by the obtaining of an acknowledgement of delivery."

- **s86(3)–(4):** failure without reasonable excuse is an offence by the company **and every officer in default**; liable on summary conviction to a fine (level 5 plus daily default fine in Scotland/NI).
- **PO boxes are prohibited.** GOV.UK: "You can no longer use a Royal Mail PO Box as your registered office address," and the restriction extends to "similar services from other companies." A pure mail-forwarding or unattended box fails limb (b) because no acknowledgement of delivery can be obtained. https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/company-address
- **Third-party/agent addresses remain permitted** — including "a service provider's address (for example, an accountant or solicitor)" — but the address "must meet all of these requirements," and the company must have the provider's permission.
- Consequence of failure: the registrar may move the company to a **default address** (s86(5) cross-refers to s1097A(3)(h), inserted by ECCTA) and ultimately **strike the company off**.
- Also live from 4 March 2024: a **registered email address** (not published on the register) and a **lawful purpose statement**.

**Obligations on the address provider.** Note carefully: the statutory duty in s86 falls on the **company and its officers**, not on the address provider. The provider's exposure is different and arguably worse:
1. It is a **TCSP** (reg 12(2)(c)) with full AML duties over every company at the address;
2. It must actually **receive, acknowledge and route** post so the client company can meet s86 — a passive mailbox does not;
3. Companies House can query and remove addresses, and an address with a large number of struck-off or non-compliant companies attracts direct regulatory and law-enforcement attention;
4. Delivering false or misleading information to the registrar is an offence — ECCTA significantly strengthened the registrar's powers and the false statement offences (I did not retrieve the exact renumbered sections; **verify the current s1112/s1112A wording**).

## 4. NOMINEE DIRECTOR SERVICES

### Lawful? Yes — but the label is legally meaningless

There is no UK prohibition on acting as a nominee/provided director. **However, English company law does not recognise a "nominee director" as a distinct, lower-liability category.** A nominee director is a director, full stop, and bears **the entire statutory duty set** under CA 2006 Part 10 Chapter 2:

- **s171** — act within powers / in accordance with the constitution
- **s172** — promote the success of **the company** (not the appointor)
- **s173** — **exercise independent judgment.** s173(2) provides the duty is not infringed by acting "(a) in accordance with an agreement duly entered into by the company that restricts the future exercise of discretion by its directors, or (b) in a way authorised by the company's constitution." ⚠️ **This is the provision nominee-service marketing over-reads.** It permits following an arrangement *the company itself* has properly entered into or that the *constitution* authorises. It does **not** permit a director to take instructions from a shareholder as a matter of course, and it does not displace s172 or s174.
- **s174** — reasonable care, skill and diligence (objective + subjective test)
- **s175** — avoid conflicts of interest (acute for a nominee serving the appointor)
- **s176** — not accept benefits from third parties
- **s177** — declare interest in proposed transactions

Beyond CA 2006: personal exposure to **wrongful trading** (IA 1986 s214), **disqualification** (CDDA 1986), unpaid PAYE/NIC and HMRC personal liability notices, sanctions breaches, health and safety, and criminal filing offences. GOV.UK puts it bluntly: delegating "you're still legally responsible for your company's records, accounts and performance," and "You may be fined, prosecuted or disqualified."

### Risk running the other way — the appointor

Directing the nominee risks making the Indian parent (or its officers) a **shadow director** under CA 2006 s251, in which case the general duties apply to it too so far as capable of applying (s170(5)). Marketing a nominee as "you keep control, we're just on paper" creates the shadow-directorship exposure while retaining the nominee's personal liability. Worst of both worlds.

### Transparency: nominee arrangements do not hide anyone

- **PSC register:** a nominee directorship does nothing to affect PSC registration — the ultimate individual with significant control remains registrable. A **nominee shareholder** does not become the PSC; the beneficial owner does — and providing nominee shareholders is itself TCSP activity under reg 12(2)(d)(ii).
- **Register of Overseas Entities** (Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act 2022, live 1 August 2022): if the Indian company acquires UK property, ROE registration is required, with verification by a **UK-regulated agent supervised under MLR 2017**, an agent assurance code and a verification checks statement completed no more than 3 months before registration. Non-compliance: "a fine, a prison sentence or both," plus inability to deal with the property. Registration fee £250. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/register-an-overseas-entity

### What ECCTA changed for nominees

1. **From 18 November 2025 every director must verify identity** and obtain a personal code. A nominee director is now personally, verifiably identified on the public register and is traceable across every directorship. The "invisible director" product is dead.
2. Registrar's new query/reject/remove powers and strengthened false-statement offences.
3. **Corporate directors: still permitted.** CA 2006 **s156A** ("A person may not be appointed a director of a company unless the person is a natural person"; appointment in contravention is **void**) was inserted by the **Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act 2015 s.87(4)** — not by ECCTA — and is in force **only "for specified purposes" from 4.3.2024** (SI 2024/270), i.e. the regulation-making power. The general prohibition is **not yet commenced**. Corporate directors remain lawful for now but the ban is on the roadmap; do not build a corporate-nominee product on it.

### The UK-resident director "requirement" — YOUR BELIEF IS CORRECT

**There is no UK residency or nationality requirement for a director of a UK company.** Confirmed:

- **CA 2006 s154:** a private company must have at least one director.
- **CA 2006 s155(1)** (in force): "A company must have at least one director who is a natural person." That is a *natural person* requirement, not a *resident* requirement.
- **GOV.UK, verbatim:** "**Directors do not have to live in the UK but companies must have a UK registered office address.**" https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/appoint-directors-and-company-secretaries
- Other requirements: director must be 16 or over; not disqualified; not an undischarged bankrupt (without court permission).

**This is a common mis-selling point.** Marketing a nominee director as *necessary* to incorporate or operate a UK company would be a false statement about the law. For a solicitor-connected business that is not merely a consumer-protection problem — it engages SRA Principles 2 (public trust), 4 (honesty) and 5 (integrity), and paragraph 8.8 of the Code (publicity must be "accurate and not misleading"). Note that in practice a UK bank account, HMRC registrations and some landlords may ask for a UK-based person — but that is commercial reality, not law, and must be described as such.

## 5. SRA RULES ON SEPARATE BUSINESSES

### The definition (SRA Glossary, verbatim)

> "**separate business** means, where you are an authorised body or own, manage or are employed by an authorised body, a separate business: which you own; which you are owned by; where you actively participate in the provision of its services, including where you have any direct control over the business or any indirect control over the business through another person, or which you are connected with, and which is not an authorised body, an authorised non-SRA firm, or an overseas practice"

https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/glossary/

**Application here:** the solicitor is engaged by Taylor Rose Limited (an SRA-licensed body) and owns FIAT LAW UK LTD, which is not an authorised body. **FIAT LAW is a "separate business" for SRA purposes.** (A consultant arrangement almost certainly falls within "own, manage or are employed by" — but if the consultancy is structured as a contract for services rather than employment, confirm with the SRA/Taylor Rose's COLP that the definition bites, as it should on "actively participate"/"connected with".)

### The operative rules — SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors, RELs and RFLs

**Section 5 — Referrals, introductions and separate businesses (verbatim):**

> **5.1** In respect of any referral of a client by you to another person, or of any third party who introduces business to you or with whom you share your fees, you ensure that:
> (a) clients are informed of any financial or other interest which you or your business or employer has in referring the client to another person or which an introducer has in referring the client to you;
> (b) clients are informed of any fee sharing arrangement that is relevant to their matter;
> (c) the fee sharing agreement is in writing;
> (d) you do not receive payments relating to a referral or make payments to an introducer in respect of clients who are the subject of criminal proceedings; and
> (e) any client referred by an introducer has not been acquired in a way which would breach the SRA's regulatory arrangements if the person acquiring the client were regulated by the SRA.
>
> **5.2** Where it appears to the SRA that you have made or received a referral fee, the payment will be treated as a referral fee unless you show that the payment was not made as such.
>
> **5.3** You only: (a) refer, recommend or introduce a client to a separate business; or (b) divide, or allow to be divided, a client's matter between you and a separate business; **where the client has given informed consent to your doing so.**

**5.4** additionally prohibits being a manager, employee, member or interest holder of a business whose name includes the word "solicitors", **or which "describes its work in a way that suggests it is a solicitors' firm"**, unless it is an authorised body.

**Section 8 — Client information and publicity (verbatim, the disclosure obligations):**

> **8.8** You ensure that any publicity in relation to your practice is accurate and not misleading…
>
> **8.10** You ensure that clients understand whether and how the services you provide are regulated. This includes:
> (a) explaining which activities will be carried out by you, as an authorised person;
> (b) explaining which services provided by you, your business or employer, **and any separate business** are regulated by an approved regulator; and
> (c) **ensuring that you do not represent any business or employer which is not authorised by the SRA, including any separate business, as being regulated by the SRA.**
>
> **8.11** You ensure that clients understand the regulatory protections available to them.

https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/code-conduct-solicitors/

**SRA Code of Conduct for Firms, paragraph 7.1** applies to Taylor Rose "in their entirety" the Solicitors Code paragraphs on "referrals, introductions and separate businesses (**5.1 to 5.3**)" and "client information and publicity (**8.6 to 8.11**)". So **Taylor Rose itself** carries these obligations, not just the individual. Firms Code paragraph 8.1: "If you are a manager, you are responsible for compliance by your firm with this Code."

### What this means operationally

1. **Both directions of referral require informed consent** (5.3) and disclosure of the solicitor's financial interest (5.1(a)). "Informed consent" is a higher bar than notification — the client must understand what they are giving up.
2. **Every client of FIAT LAW must be told, in terms, that FIAT LAW is not SRA-regulated** and what that means (8.10, 8.11): no SRA Compensation Fund, no Legal Ombudsman, no client account protections, no SRA minimum-terms PII, and **legal professional privilege will generally not attach** to advice given by a non-lawyer entity (*Prudential plc v Special Commissioner of Income Tax* [2013] UKSC 1 — privilege is confined to advice from members of the legal profession). For Indian clients buying "legal services," loss of privilege is a material and often decisive disclosure.
3. **The name and marketing are a live 5.4 risk.** "FIAT LAW UK LTD" marketing "legal services to startups" while owned by a practising solicitor is squarely in the territory of a business that "describes its work in a way that suggests it is a solicitors' firm." Also consider Solicitors Act 1974 s21 (wilfully pretending to be a solicitor) and LSA 2007 s17 (offence of pretending to be entitled to carry on a reserved activity). **This should be reviewed by independent regulatory counsel before the package launches.** I regard it as the highest-probability enforcement exposure in the whole structure.
4. **What can be delivered through FIAT LAW:** any *non-reserved* service — company formation, registered office, accounting, general commercial advisory — provided FIAT LAW holds the sector-specific authorisations set out elsewhere in this report.
5. **What cannot:** all six reserved legal activities (section 6 below), and immigration advice without IAA registration (section 7).
6. Note that the 2019 Standards and Regulations **replaced** the far more prescriptive separate-business rules in Chapter 12 of the 2011 SRA Handbook with the principles-based 5.3/8.10 approach. ⚠️ I assert that from background knowledge and could not source a specific SRA statement confirming it — **verify if the historical position matters**. What is certain is that the *current* rules are as quoted above.
7. **Taylor Rose's consent.** Consultant agreements at ABS platforms almost invariably require disclosure and approval of outside business interests, and Taylor Rose's COLP has firm-level exposure under Firms Code 7.1. This needs to be squared with Taylor Rose in writing, not assumed.

## 6. RESERVED LEGAL ACTIVITIES — LSA 2007

**Section 12(1) LSA 2007** — the six reserved legal activities, defined in Schedule 2:

1. **Rights of audience** — appearing before and addressing a court, including calling and examining witnesses
2. **Conduct of litigation** — issuing, commencing, prosecuting and defending proceedings, and ancillary functions
3. **Reserved instrument activities** — preparing instruments of transfer or charge for Land Registration Act 2002 purposes; lodging/applying for registration under that Act; **preparing any other instrument relating to real or personal estate, or relating to court proceedings**. Sch 2 expressly excludes wills and other testamentary instruments, letters/powers of attorney, and agreements not intended to be executed as a deed
4. **Probate activities** — preparing probate papers
5. **Notarial activities**
6. **Administration of oaths**

Source: https://www.legalservicesboard.org.uk/for-consumers/reserved-legal-activities

### Direct answers

- **General legal advice — NOT reserved.** The LSB states plainly that advice or assistance "in connection with the application of the law or with any form of resolution of legal disputes," and representation on such matters, falls outside the six categories and needs no authorisation.
- **Contract drafting — NOT reserved**, with an important carve-out: drafting a **transfer or charge of land** for LRA 2002 purposes, or any other **instrument relating to real or personal estate**, IS reserved (Sch 2 para 5). A UK market-entry package that includes a lease, property purchase, or a debenture/security document crosses into reserved territory.
- **Immigration advice — NOT reserved** under LSA 2007, but separately regulated under a different statute (section 7 below). Do not conflate the two.
- Also **not reserved:** corporate/commercial advisory, M&A transaction documents, employment advice, tax advice, will-writing, mediation (LSA excludes judicial/quasi-judicial functions and mediation from "legal activity" altogether).

### THE CRITICAL TRAP — LSA s15(4)

**s14(1):** it is an offence to carry on a reserved legal activity unless entitled. **s14(3):** on indictment, up to **2 years' imprisonment and/or a fine**.

**s15(2)–(4)** determines who "carries on" the activity:

> "(2) References to a person carrying on an activity which is a reserved legal activity include a person ('E') who— (a) is an employee of a person ('P'), and (b) carries on the activity in E's capacity as such an employee.
> (3) For the purposes of subsection (2), **it is irrelevant whether P is entitled to carry on the activity.**
> (4) **P does not carry on an activity … which is a reserved legal activity by virtue of E carrying it on in E's capacity as an employee of P, unless the provision of relevant services to the public or a section of the public (with or without a view to profit) is part of P's business.**"

s15(11): "If P is a body, references to an employee of P include references to a **manager** of P."

**Consequence:** FIAT LAW **cannot** lawfully deliver reserved legal activities even by using the solicitor's own authorisation, because supplying such services to the public would be "part of P's business" and FIAT LAW itself would then be carrying on the reserved activity unauthorised — an offence under s14. The solicitor, as a manager of FIAT LAW, is caught by s15(11).

**Therefore:** all reserved work must be **contracted with, retained by, billed by and delivered through Taylor Rose Limited**, on a Taylor Rose retainer, in Taylor Rose's name, on Taylor Rose's terms. FIAT LAW may introduce the client (subject to Code 5.1/5.3) but must not sit in the contractual chain for reserved work.

## 7. IMMIGRATION ADVICE

### The prohibition

**Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 s84(1):** "No person may provide immigration advice or immigration services unless he is a qualified person."

**s84(2) — qualified persons:**
- (a) a **registered person** (registered with the Commissioner/IAA);
- (b) authorised by a **designated professional body**;
- (ba) authorised by a **designated qualifying regulator**;
- (e) a person "**acting on behalf of, and under the supervision of**, a person within any of paragraphs (a) to (ba) (whether or not under a contract of employment)."

**s86A(2) — the designated qualifying regulators are:** "(a) the Law Society; (b) the Institute of Legal Executives; (c) the General Council of the Bar."

**s84(3A)(b):** entitlement via route (ba) "does not extend to the provision of such advice or services by the person **other than in England and Wales**" (regardless of where the client is).

### Answers

- **Is an SRA-regulated solicitor exempt from IAA registration? YES.** The Law Society is a designated qualifying regulator under s86A(2)(a) and the SRA is its regulatory arm. A solicitor authorised by the SRA is a "qualified person" under s84(2)(ba) and needs no IAA registration — subject to the SRA's own regulatory arrangements and the England-and-Wales territorial limit in s84(3A)(b). **Taylor Rose can do immigration work.**
- **Would FIAT LAW need IAA registration? YES, if it provides immigration advice or services in its own name.** FIAT LAW is not authorised by any designated qualifying regulator and is not otherwise exempt. The only alternatives are (i) IAA registration at the appropriate level, or (ii) reliance on s84(2)(e) — which turns on the *individual adviser* genuinely acting on behalf of and under the supervision of a qualified person. **Running an immigration-advice business line through an unregulated company under s84(2)(e) is high-risk and I would not recommend it**; the clean route is to deliver immigration work through Taylor Rose.
- **Offence — s91(1):** providing immigration advice or services in contravention of s84 is an offence, liable **on summary conviction to up to 6 months and/or a fine up to the statutory maximum; on indictment to up to 2 years and/or a fine.** **s91(3)–(4):** where the offence is committed by a body corporate with the consent or connivance of, or attributable to the neglect of, "a director, manager, secretary or other similar officer," **that officer is personally guilty** as well as the company. **A solicitor-director of FIAT LAW would be personally exposed.**

### The regulator's name — CONFIRMED CHANGE

**The regulator is now the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA).** GOV.UK's organisation page states "Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner is now called Immigration Advice Authority," and the public-facing "Find an immigration adviser" page says "All immigration advisers must be registered with IAA or be a member of an approved professional body." The statutory office-holder is still the **Immigration Services Commissioner** (currently Gaon Hart), and the underlying legislation (IAA 1999 Part V) still uses "the Commissioner"; legacy `oisc.gov.uk` URLs remain in use.

⚠️ **I could not establish an exact rename date.** The earliest IAA-branded GOV.UK publications are dated **16 January 2025**. Note also an **IAA factsheet dated 4 December 2025 on the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025** — that Act may have amended Part V (the legislation.gov.uk annotations show 2025 c. 31 Sch. 1 amending Schedule 6 of the 1999 Act). **Verify whether the 2025 Act changed the registration or exemption framework** before advising.

⚠️ **Grey area to flag:** "immigration advice" under IAA 1999 s82 turns on advice relating to **a particular individual**. Whether **sponsor licence** work for a corporate client (a core UK market-entry service for Indian companies) constitutes regulated immigration advice is contested and fact-sensitive. The IAA has historically taken an expansive view. **Check current IAA guidance directly** — this matters commercially because sponsor licensing is likely to be the highest-volume immigration service in the package.

## 8. ACCOUNTING SERVICES

### Can an unregulated company provide bookkeeping/accounting/payroll? Yes

There is **no reserved-activity or general licensing barrier**. Accounting, bookkeeping, management accounts, tax return preparation and payroll are not reserved legal activities and are not otherwise a licensed profession in the UK. Anyone can trade as an "accountant." Protected titles ("chartered accountant", "chartered certified accountant") require the relevant body's membership.

### But AML supervision is mandatory

FIAT LAW would be an **"accountancy service provider"** (MLR 2017 reg 11 / reg 3 "external accountant"). Per HMRC guidance, in scope: "professional bookkeeping services," accounts preparation and signing, client-specific tax advice, assistance completing/submitting returns, and "payroll agents that provide accountancy services or tax advice." Delivery "virtually or through an automated service" does not remove the obligation. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-accountancy-service-provider-registration

- **Supervisor:** a professional body if a member (ICAEW, ACCA, ICAS, CAI, AAT, AIA, ATT, CIMA, CIOT, ICB, IFA, IAB, the Law Society, FCA) — "If you're supervised by one of these bodies, you do not need to register with HMRC." **Otherwise HMRC.** FIAT LAW has no professional body membership → **HMRC**.
- **No ICAEW/ACCA membership is required** to provide the services — only to use protected titles or do reserved/licensed work.
- **Payroll-only exclusions:** a payroll bureau is out of scope if it *only* provides software/hardware support without analysing or preparing financial information, or pays invoiced fees, or operates as an umbrella/managed service company, or runs payroll as "a small part of your main business activity" alongside recruitment/HR. Bundling payroll with tax advice defeats the exclusion.
- **Approval check fee £40 per person** for the accountancy sector (versus £500 fit-and-proper for TCSP). The same HMRC registration covers multiple sectors; **the £500 TCSP fit-and-proper test will apply because of the TCSP activity**.
- Unlike TCSP, accountancy-sector businesses **may trade while the application is being processed** (MLR reg 56(2) lists the sectors that may not — TCSPs and MSBs must wait). **But because FIAT LAW will be a TCSP, it must wait for approval before trading in any event.**

### Statutory audit — CONFIRMED SEPARATE AND RESTRICTED

Statutory audit is **restricted** under Companies Act 2006 Part 42. A firm must be registered with a **Recognised Supervisory Body** — **ACCA, ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland** (AIA, CIPFA and CIMA cannot register auditors). At least one partner/principal/director must be a qualified auditor, PII is required, and the firm itself must be a registered audit firm.

GOV.UK, verbatim: "**It's illegal to audit if you're not a registered auditor and you could be prosecuted.**" https://www.gov.uk/find-licences/become-a-registered-auditor

⚠️ I did not verify the specific CA 2006 section numbers (I believe the offence provisions sit around ss.1213/1250 in Part 42) — **confirm section references before citing them.**

**Other restricted adjacencies worth naming in the compliance manual:** insolvency practice (licence required, IA 1986 ss.388–390); probate (reserved, LSA Sch 2); and the protected accountancy titles.

## 9. PROFESSIONAL INDEMNITY INSURANCE — THE GAP IS REAL

### Taylor Rose's SRA policy will not cover FIAT LAW work

**SRA Indemnity Insurance Rules:**
- **Rule 1.1:** the rules "apply to authorised bodies and their principals." The introduction states they "do not apply to solicitors, RELs, RFLs and RSLs that practise outside SRA authorised firms."
- **Rule 2.1:** an authorised body must take out and maintain qualifying insurance with a participating insurer.
- **Rule 3.1:** overarching duty to hold "adequate and appropriate cover in respect of current or past practice."

**Minimum Terms and Conditions, Annex 1 clause 1** — the insuring clause is **doubly limited**: the policy "must indemnify each insured against civil liability **to the extent that it arises from private legal practice**," and that liability must be "**in connection with the insured firm's practice**" (including prior and successor practice).

**Glossary, "private legal practice"** = "the provision of services in private practice as a solicitor, REL or RSL **in an authorised body**…"

**Conclusion: services provided by FIAT LAW UK LTD, contracted with FIAT LAW, billed by FIAT LAW, are not "private legal practice … in an authorised body" and are not "in connection with the insured firm's practice." They fall outside Taylor Rose's minimum-terms cover.** Minimum cover for a licensed body is £3m per claim with unlimited defence costs and six-year run-off — none of which reaches the FIAT LAW work.

⚠️ **One nuance requiring specific verification.** MTC clause **1.3(b)** brings within the definition of "insured" "each **service, administration, trustee or nominee company** owned by the insured firm and/or its principals." FIAT LAW is owned by an individual solicitor who is a *consultant* at Taylor Rose (likely not a "principal"), and FIAT LAW is a trading company, not a service/nominee company. Even if 1.3(b) applied, it only makes an entity *an insured*; the insuring clause still requires the liability to arise from private legal practice in connection with the insured firm's practice. **Do not rely on 1.3(b). Get written confirmation from Taylor Rose's broker and insurer on where the boundary falls, and whether the arrangement must be disclosed to insurers as a material fact — non-disclosure of a connected unregulated trading entity is itself a risk to the firm's cover.**

### The gaps to plug

| Exposure | Cover needed |
|---|---|
| Non-reserved advisory, formation, company secretarial errors by FIAT LAW | Standalone commercial PI for FIAT LAW (SRA minimum terms are far broader than standard commercial PI — no avoidance, no retroactive date, unlimited defence costs, 6-year run-off; a commercial policy will have none of these) |
| **Nominee/provided directorship** | **D&O cover for the individual acting as director of each client company — and note that "rent-a-director"/nominee exposure is a standard exclusion or heavy rating factor in the D&O market.** This is often uninsurable in practice at sensible cost. Confirm before offering the service. |
| Accounting/payroll errors | Accountants' PI |
| Run-off | Commercial PI is typically claims-made with no mandatory run-off — a real long-tail problem on directorships |

### The client-protection gap (must be disclosed under Code 8.11)

Clients of FIAT LAW get **none** of: SRA Compensation Fund access, Legal Ombudsman jurisdiction, SRA minimum-terms PII, client account/SRA Accounts Rules protection, or (per *Prudential* [2013] UKSC 1) legal professional privilege over the advice.

## ADDITIONAL ISSUE NOT IN THE BRIEF — (f) M&A ADVISORY AND FSMA

I flag this because it is the largest unasked-about risk.

Advising on, or arranging deals in, **shares** are regulated activities (Regulated Activities Order 2001, arts 25 and 53). Carrying them on by way of business without authorisation or exemption breaches the **general prohibition (FSMA 2000 s19)**, which is a **criminal offence (s23)** and renders resulting agreements **unenforceable (ss.26–27)**.

- **Exclusion — RAO art 70** ("Activities carried on in connection with the sale of a body corporate"). Verbatim conditions in art 70(2): "(a) the shares consist of or include **50 per cent or more of the voting shares** in the body corporate; or (b) the shares, together with any already held … consist of or include at least that percentage; and (c) in either case, the acquisition or disposal is **between parties each of whom is a body corporate, a partnership, a single individual or a group of connected individuals**." Art 70(1)(b)(ii) alternatively allows the exclusion where "the object of the transaction may nevertheless reasonably be regarded as being the acquisition of day to day control of the affairs of the body corporate." https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/70
  ⚠️ I read art 70(1)–(3) only. **Art 70 extends to the agency/arranging/advising articles in later paragraphs — verify paragraphs (4)–(6) apply to arts 21, 25 and 53 for your intended activity.** Minority-stake, growth-equity and fundraising work will typically fall **outside** art 70.
- **Taylor Rose has a route FIAT LAW does not:** the **FSMA Part XX exempt professional firm regime** (ss.325–333), under which the SRA is a designated professional body and the SRA Financial Services (Scope) Rules apply. **FIAT LAW cannot use Part XX at all.**
- Fundraising/capital introduction for Indian companies raising in the UK would also engage the **financial promotion restriction (FSMA s21)**.

**Recommendation: take specialist FSMA advice before offering M&A advisory through the unregulated entity.**
